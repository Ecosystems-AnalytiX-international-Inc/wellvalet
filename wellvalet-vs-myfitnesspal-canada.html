WellValet vs MyFitnessPal: Which Is Right for Canadian Grocery Shoppers?

MyFitnessPal moved its barcode scanner behind a paywall back in 2022. Here's an honest look at how WellValet compares for Canadian shoppers who want a free scanner, real allergen alerts, and PIPEDA-compliant privacy.

Home › Resources › WellValet vs MyFitnessPal Canada

MyFitnessPal and WellValet get compared a lot, but they're not actually built to do the same job — one is a calorie and macro tracker, the other is a wellness and allergen-safety scanner. Where the comparison actually matters is at the point millions of former MyFitnessPal users hit in 2022 and are still hitting today: the barcode scanner, once free, now sits behind a Premium subscription.

Below is an honest, feature-by-feature look — including where WellValet doesn't try to be MyFitnessPal.

Note on that last row: we're not going to pretend WellValet replaces a dedicated calorie-counting app. If detailed macro logging against a huge existing food diary is what you need, MyFitnessPal (paywall aside) genuinely does that well. WellValet solves a different, narrower problem: what's actually in this product, and is it safe for the allergies and preferences your family has told us about.

Why the Barcode Scanner Comparison Matters

1. The Scanner Itself Isn't Behind a Paywall

MyFitnessPal began moving its barcode scanner behind Premium in late 2022, and as of 2026 that remains the standard experience for most users and new sign-ups — Premium runs US$19.99/month or US$79.99/year just to unlock it, on top of everything else. WellValet's barcode scanner and OCR ingredient scanning are free, full stop, with no subscription required to use them.

2. Personalized Allergen Alerts vs. Generic Macro Tracking

MyFitnessPal will tell you a product's calories and macros. It won't check that product against a family member's peanut allergy. WellValet was built around the opposite priority: set up your allergen profile once, and every scan gets checked against it — including for the hidden allergens grocery shopping still doesn't protect you from.

3. Zero Ads, Even on the Free Tier

MyFitnessPal's free tier carries ads throughout the app, alongside the barcode-scanner paywall. WellValet has no ads at any tier, free or paid — because the business model isn't built around ad impressions or upsell friction.

4. Canadian-First, Not a Retrofitted U.S. Database

MyFitnessPal's food database is large but built around U.S. products and U.S. privacy norms. WellValet indexes Canadian store brands specifically, and complies with PIPEDA rather than a U.S. privacy framework. See our research testing four product databases for Canadian grocery coverage for exactly how we validated that.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is MyFitnessPal's barcode scanner free in Canada?

For most users, no. MyFitnessPal moved barcode scanning behind Premium starting in late 2022, and as of 2026 the free tier requires manual food search instead. Some legacy accounts in a few countries reportedly retain free access, but it isn't the standard experience for new sign-ups. WellValet's scanner stays free at every tier.

Does MyFitnessPal check for food allergens?

No — it's built for calorie and macro tracking, not allergen safety, and has no feature that checks a scan against a saved allergen profile the way WellValet does.

Can WellValet replace MyFitnessPal for calorie counting?

Not directly, and we'd rather tell you that upfront than oversell it. MyFitnessPal is purpose-built for macro logging against a huge food diary database. WellValet is purpose-built for wellness scoring, allergen alerts, and ingredient safety — different core jobs, with some shoppers reasonably wanting both.
