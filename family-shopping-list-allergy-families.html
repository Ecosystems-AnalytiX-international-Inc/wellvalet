How to Build a Family Shopping List That Works for Allergy Families

14 November 2026 · 6 min read

Most family shopping lists live in three places at once: a note on someone's phone, a photo of the fridge whiteboard, and a group chat where "we're out of milk" gets buried under everything else. For most households that means a second trip to the store. For an allergy family it can mean coming home with the wrong snack, the one with the "may contain" line nobody had time to read.

A good shopping list fixes that. Here is what one looks like, and how to set one up in WellValet so everyone in the family can add what they need and the person doing the shopping has it all in one place.

What makes a shopping list actually work

  • Everyone adds, one person sees it all. Each family member records what they need, and the person doing the shopping sees it together, so nothing lives in a side chat or on a sticky note.
  • Specific items, not vague ones. "Oat milk, unsweetened, 1.75 L" beats "milk", especially when only one product has been checked for your family's allergies.
  • Allergy context travels with the list. Whoever is shopping needs to know who an item is for and what to avoid, even if they are not the person who usually shops.
  • Nobody's request gets lost. Items go straight onto the list instead of into a message thread, so the shopper is not relying on memory.
  • It starts from your meals. Planning the week's meals first means the list covers what you will actually cook.

Setting it up in WellValet, step by step

  1. Give every family member their own profile. Each person gets their own allergy and dietary profile, so when you scan a product, WellValet flags what is unsafe for your child, not just for you.
  2. Create your family group and invite everyone. On the Family plan, the family admin shares a 6-digit invite code. Each member opens WellValet, goes to Profile, taps "Join Family Group" and enters the code. They are added immediately. A family group can have up to 4 members.
  3. Plan the week, then build the list. Use the Weekly Meal Planner for ideas, with breakfast, lunch, dinner and snack suggestions based on your purchase history. Then each family member adds the items they need to the Shopping List in the app.
  4. Shop from the admin's combined list. Each member sees the items they have added, and the family admin sees everyone's items in one list. Whoever shops with the admin's list has the whole family's needs in one place. No more "did anyone ask for bread?" messages.
  5. Scan before it goes in the cart. For anything new, or anything whose recipe might have changed, scan the barcode. WellValet checks the ingredients against every family member's saved profile in seconds.
Why scan products you have bought before? Manufacturers change recipes. A product that suited your family last month may carry a new ingredient or a new "may contain" line today, and a quick scan at the shelf catches it. Here is what "may contain" actually means in Canada.

Practical tips for allergy households

  • Name the exact product and size for items you have already checked, so a different flavour or pack size does not slip into the cart.
  • Say who an item is for in the item name, for example "Granola bars (for Sam, nut-free)", so whoever shops knows why it matters.
  • Add party, school and lunchbox items early in the week. They are the ones most often bought in a hurry.
  • Plan the big shop around the admin's list. The family admin is the only one who sees everyone's items, so the weekly shop goes most smoothly when it starts from that list.
  • Treat "may contain" as a reason to put the product back, in line with Food Allergy Canada's advice, and contact the manufacturer if you are unsure.
